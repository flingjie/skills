# Question Bank

Organized by interview phase and scenario. These are templates — adapt them to the user's specific context. The power is in the follow-up, not the first question.

## Phase 1: Context Questions

### Narrowing the scope

- "Who specifically experiences this? Give me a role, a company size, an industry — not 'everyone.'"
- "When you picture the person dealing with this, what are they doing right before it happens?"
- "What's the trigger? Is it an event, a deadline, a certain type of task?"
- "How many people like this do you know personally?"

### Frequency anchoring

- "Looking at your calendar from last month — how many times did this come up?"
- "When was the most recent time? What about the time before that?"
- "Is this a daily annoyance, a weekly fire, or a quarterly headache?"
- "Does it happen on a schedule (every Monday, every month-end) or randomly?"

## Phase 2: Current Behavior Questions

### Mapping the workflow

- "Walk me through the last time this happened. Start from the moment you realized you needed to deal with it."
- "What's the first thing you open? The first person you message?"
- "Show me — what does the screen look like? What file? What tool?"
- "How many steps from start to done? How many minutes?"
- "Who else touches this before it's resolved?"

### Finding existing solutions

- "What are you using today? Even if it's a messy spreadsheet."
- "Have you tried anything else? What happened?"
- "Why did you stop using [previous attempt]?"
- "Is there a workaround everyone on the team knows about but nobody talks about?"

### For automation/agent ideas

- "Who does this work today? Is it one person's full job, or something everyone does on the side?"
- "Is there a written process for this? A checklist? An SOP?"
- "Has anyone on the team tried to automate this — a script, a Zapier flow, a macro?"
- "What's the hardest part to automate? The part that needs human judgment?"
- "What happens when this doesn't get done? Who notices first?"

## Phase 3: Pain Evaluation Questions

### Probing severity

- "What's the worst part? The thing that makes you sigh when you have to do it."
- "What happens if you just... don't do it? Who gets upset?"
- "How much time are we talking, in a typical week?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand and fix this, what would you do with the time you get back?"
- "Is this a top-3 annoyance in your workday, or more like top-20?"

### Probing cost

- "Does this cost money directly — tools, services, contractors?"
- "If you had to put a dollar figure on the time spent, what would it be?"
- "Has anyone ever missed a deadline or lost a customer because of this?"
- "Is there a budget for solving this? Who controls it?"

### Commitment signals

- "Have you ever paid for anything that partially solved this?"
- "Did you or your team ever build something internally to deal with this?"
- "Has this come up in a team retro or planning meeting?"
- "Would solving this make someone look good at their job? Get them promoted?"

## Phase 4: Solution Questions (only after validation)

### Exploring solution space

- "What's the smallest thing that would help? Not the dream version — the v0."
- "If you could change one step in the current process, which one?"
- "What would make you switch from what you're doing now?"
- "What's your biggest worry about adopting a new tool for this?"

### For AI/automation ideas

- "What part of this needs human judgment, and what part is pure repetition?"
- "If an AI did 80% of the work, would the remaining 20% still be worth it?"
- "What's the cost of getting it wrong? Is a mistake embarrassing, or catastrophic?"
- "Would you trust an automated system with this, or would you need to review every output?"

### Willingness to pay

- "Not 'how much would you pay' — but: is there a budget line item this would fit under?"
- "What tools are you already paying for that touch this problem?"
- "Who signs off on tool purchases in this area? What's their threshold?"

## Anti-Patterns to Watch For

### The "everyone" problem
> "Everyone needs this."
→ "Who is the first person? What's their name? What company?"

### The "someday" problem
> "Companies will need this when [future trend]."
→ "What are they doing today? Is anyone asking for this now?"

### The "better mousetrap" problem
> "Existing tools are bad, we'll make a better one."
→ "Who is so unhappy with the existing tool that they're actively looking to switch? How do you know?"

### The "LLM can do it" problem
> "Now that LLMs exist, we can automate this."
→ "Why couldn't this be automated before? What specifically makes LLMs the unlock — and what makes the previous constraint the bottleneck, not something else?"

### The "scratch my own itch" fallacy
> "I need this, so others must too."
→ "Are you the exception or the rule? What makes you similar to the target user? What makes you different?"
