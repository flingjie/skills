# Commercial Insight

The commercial insight is the engine of challenger selling. It is NOT a catchy
headline or a generic truth. It is a specific claim about the customer's world
that they have not yet articulated.

## Formula

```
Insight =
  Customer Situation
  + Unexpected Problem
  + Business Consequence
  + New Perspective
```

## Template

> 很多 [同类客户]
>
> 认为 [当前认知]
>
> 但实际上
>
> [隐藏机制]
>
> 正在导致
>
> [业务后果]
>
> 因此真正应该解决的不是
>
> [旧问题]
>
> 而是
>
> [新问题]

## Worked example

> 很多团队认为 Agent 项目的主要问题是模型准确率，但实际导致 Agent 无法规模化的，
> 通常不是模型能力，而是团队无法系统记录、分类和复现 Agent 的失败模式。
>
> 因此真正的问题不是"如何继续优化 Prompt"，而是"如何建立 Agent 的 Evaluation
> 和 Learning Loop"。

## Specificity rules

An insight must name:

1. **Specific mechanism** — the actual chain of cause and effect.
2. **Specific consequence** — a measurable or observable result.
3. **Specific new perspective** — a different problem definition.

### Banned (too generic)

- "AI is changing everything."
- "Efficiency is important."
- "Automation can save costs."

These carry no mechanism, no consequence, and no new perspective — they are
claims anyone could make about anything.

### Good test

If you can swap the product name out and the sentence still makes sense, it is
not specific enough. A real insight survives on its mechanism, not on the
product's branding.

## Where the insight comes from

It is built from Step 3 (assumptions). The insight is the payoff of finding an
assumption that is wrong. If you cannot point to a specific assumption you are
challenging, you do not yet have an insight — you have a marketing line.

## Distinguish fact from hypothesis

State the customer's situation and consequence as facts when the customer gave
them; state the hidden mechanism as a hypothesis when you are inferring it.
The narrative must be able to survive the customer saying "actually, that's not
our situation" — so anchor it in what they told you, and test the hypothesis
with questions before asserting it.
