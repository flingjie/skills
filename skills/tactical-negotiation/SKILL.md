# Tactical Negotiation

Use this skill when the user needs help with:

- Sales objections
- Price negotiations
- Salary negotiations
- Client negotiations
- Conflict resolution
- Difficult conversations
- Stakeholder management
- Persuasion
- Handling resistance
- Discovering hidden motivations or constraints

The goal is NOT to win an argument.

The goal is to understand the other side's world well enough to uncover
the real problem and jointly construct a better outcome.

---

# Core Operating Principle

Never move directly from:

statement → solution

Instead follow:

```text
statement
    ↓
emotion
    ↓
motivation
    ↓
constraint
    ↓
hidden information
    ↓
shared problem definition
    ↓
solution
```

Do not immediately:

- defend
- explain
- justify
- persuade
- discount
- compromise

First understand.

---

# Conversation Analysis Model

For every important statement from the other party, analyze:

## 1. Surface Statement

What did they literally say?

Example:

> "Your price is too high."

Do not assume this is the real problem.

---

## 2. Emotion

What emotional state may be present?

Possible categories:

- fear
- uncertainty
- frustration
- distrust
- pressure
- urgency
- loss aversion
- desire for control

Example:

> Fear of making another bad purchasing decision.

---

## 3. Motivation

What outcome do they want?

Examples:

- reduce risk
- save money
- gain control
- avoid blame
- get promoted
- finish quickly
- protect reputation

---

## 4. Constraint

What limits their options?

Examples:

- budget
- approval process
- deadline
- internal politics
- procurement rules
- management expectations
- previous failures

---

## 5. Black Swan Hypothesis

What important information might be hidden?

Examples:

- They already have a preferred vendor.
- They have unused budget.
- Their manager is blocking the decision.
- A previous project failed.
- The deadline is artificial.
- They are under internal pressure.

Do not assume the hypothesis is true.

Use questions to test it.

---

# Response Strategy

Before proposing a solution, choose the appropriate tactic.

Possible tactics:

1. Mirror
2. Label
3. Accusation Audit
4. Summary
5. Calibrated Question
6. No-oriented Question
7. Silence
8. Information Discovery

Use the minimum number of tactics necessary.

Do not stack multiple techniques mechanically.

---

# 1. Mirror

Repeat one to three important words from the other person's statement.

Purpose:

Encourage elaboration without interrogation.

Example:

Other person:

> "We don't have the budget."

Response:

> "Don't have the budget?"

Then stop.

Do not immediately add another question.

Good mirrors often target:

- emotionally loaded phrases
- ambiguous phrases
- important constraints
- unusual wording

---

# 2. Label

Identify and verbalize the other person's possible emotion,
motivation, or perception.

Preferred patterns:

- "It sounds like..."
- "It seems like..."
- "It looks like..."
- "听起来……"
- "感觉……"
- "似乎……"

Examples:

> "听起来你最大的担心不是价格本身，而是这个投入是否值得。"

> "感觉之前的经历让你对类似项目比较谨慎。"

A label should be:

- tentative
- non-judgmental
- short
- emotionally accurate

Avoid:

> "You are wrong."

Prefer:

> "It sounds like this doesn't feel safe enough yet."

---

# 3. Accusation Audit

Before the other party raises an obvious negative perception,
state it first.

Use when distrust or defensiveness is likely.

Examples:

> "你可能会觉得我只是想推动你尽快购买。"

> "你可能会担心我们只是换一种方式向你推销。"

> "从你的角度看，这可能和你之前失败的项目没有什么区别。"

Do not defend yourself immediately afterward.

Allow the other person to respond.

---

# 4. Summary

Summarize the other person's world.

Include:

- facts
- emotions
- motivations
- constraints

Structure:

```text
What happened
+
What matters to them
+
What they are worried about
```

Example:

> "如果我理解正确，你们并不是完全不愿意做这个项目。
> 只是之前的项目效果不好，现在管理层对类似投入比较谨慎。
> 所以你们最重要的不是找到最低价格，而是确保这次不会再次失败。"

The objective is to reach:

> "That's right."

Do not confuse:

> "You're right."

with genuine alignment.

---

# 5. Calibrated Questions

Use open questions that force joint problem-solving.

Preferred question types:

## What

Examples:

* "What is most important here?"
* "What would success look like?"
* "What is preventing this from moving forward?"
* "什么条件满足以后，你会认为这个方案值得推进？"

## How

Examples:

* "How would you like us to solve that?"
* "How can we reduce that risk?"
* "How am I supposed to do that?"

Example:

Other party:

> "You need to reduce the price by 50%."

Instead of:

> "That's impossible."

Use:

> "我理解你需要控制预算。我们怎样才能在保持交付质量的情况下做到这一点？"

This converts:

conflict

into:

joint problem-solving.

---

# 6. No-Oriented Questions

Do not always chase "Yes."

A strategic "No" can give the other party:

* control
* safety
* autonomy

Examples:

Instead of:

> "Is now a good time?"

Use:

> "现在讨论这个问题会不会不太方便？"

Instead of:

> "Would you like to explore this?"

Use:

> "你是否觉得现在还不适合讨论这个方案？"

Do not overuse this technique.

---

# 7. Silence

After:

* a mirror
* a label
* a difficult question
* a proposal

Do not rush to fill the silence.

Silence creates space for:

* thinking
* correction
* disclosure
* elaboration

# Negotiation State Machine

Track the conversation as a state machine.

```text
SURFACE_POSITION
      ↓
EMOTION_DISCOVERY
      ↓
MOTIVATION_DISCOVERY
      ↓
CONSTRAINT_DISCOVERY
      ↓
BLACK_SWAN_DISCOVERY
      ↓
SHARED_UNDERSTANDING
      ↓
OPTION_GENERATION
      ↓
AGREEMENT_DESIGN
```

Do not jump directly from:

SURFACE_POSITION

to:

OPTION_GENERATION

unless the user's goal is simply to generate a quick response.

---

# Decision Framework

When the other party says something important:

## Step 1

Extract:

```text
Statement:
Emotion:
Motivation:
Constraint:
Unknown:
Black Swan Hypothesis:
```

## Step 2

Determine:

What do we NOT know yet?

Prefer discovering unknown information over presenting arguments.

## Step 3

Choose one next move:

```text
MIRROR
LABEL
SUMMARY
CALIBRATED_QUESTION
ACCUSATION_AUDIT
NO_QUESTION
SILENCE
```

## Step 4

Generate the response.

Do not generate more than two consecutive questions.

Prefer:

understanding → response

over:

question → question → question → question

---

# Output Format

When analyzing a negotiation, use:

## Situation

Brief description.

## Surface Position

What the other party explicitly says.

## Likely Underlying Factors

| Type                | Analysis |
| ------------------- | -------- |
| Emotion             |          |
| Motivation          |          |
| Constraint          |          |
| Unknown             |          |
| Possible Black Swan |          |

Clearly distinguish facts from hypotheses.

## Recommended Next Move

Choose ONE:

* Mirror
* Label
* Summary
* Calibrated Question
* Accusation Audit
* No-oriented Question

Explain briefly why.

## Suggested Response

Provide a natural conversational response.

Avoid sounding like a negotiation textbook.

# Conversation Simulation Mode

When the user wants to practice negotiation:

1. Create a realistic scenario.
2. Play the counterpart.
3. Do not reveal hidden motivations immediately.
4. Maintain internal state:

```text
trust
information_disclosed
resistance
urgency
negotiation_power
```

5. Respond naturally.
6. After each user response:

Provide:

```text
OBSERVATION
- What worked
- What was missed

COUNTERPART STATE
- Trust: ↑ / ↓ / →
- Information revealed
- Remaining unknowns

NEXT OPPORTUNITY
- Possible Mirror
- Possible Label
- Possible Question
```

Then continue the roleplay.

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

## 1. Immediate Solutioning

Bad:

> "价格高？我们可以给你打折。"

Better:

> "听起来价格只是表面问题。"

---

## 2. Argument Mode

Bad:

> "我们的产品确实比竞争对手好。"

Better:

> "感觉你现在还没有看到这个差异值得支付更多成本。"

---

## 3. Fake Empathy

Avoid:

> "我完全理解你的感受。"

Unless you actually demonstrate understanding.

Prefer:

> "听起来你最担心的是……"

---

## 4. Question Machine

Do not interrogate:

> 为什么？
> 为什么？
> 为什么？
> 为什么？

Alternate:

```text
Mirror
↓
Silence
↓
Label
↓
Question
↓
Summary
```

---

## 5. Premature Compromise

Do not assume:

```text
A = 100
B = 60

Solution = 80
```

Instead explore:

* scope
* timing
* risk
* payment structure
* guarantees
* success metrics
* phased commitments

Redesign the deal before splitting the difference.

---

# Final Principle

The best negotiation move is often not a better argument.

It is a better understanding of the other person's world.

Your objective is:

NOT

"How do I convince them?"

BUT

"What must be true in their world for this position to make sense?"

Then discover what you do not yet know.
